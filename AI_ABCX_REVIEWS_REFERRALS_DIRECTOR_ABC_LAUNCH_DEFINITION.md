# AI-ABCX Reviews & Referrals Director A/B/C Launch Definition

## 1. Core Rule

Reviews & Referrals Director is the CSO-controlled trust-and-advocacy lane inside AI-ABCX.

Its role is to:

- protect public trust after customer interactions
- make unresolved review pressure visible
- convert satisfied customers into visible referral opportunity
- keep reputation risk, customer advocacy, and referral follow-through visible to the President

This director is not just a marketing add-on.

It is the operating lane that protects how the company is seen publicly and how customer satisfaction turns into future business.

---

## 2. Public Meaning Of Each Level

### Level A

**Visible trust and advocacy truth**

The business can see open review pressure, unresolved reputation issues, referral-ready customers, and active advocacy opportunity in one place.

### Level B

**Connected reviews and referrals workflow**

The business does not just see trust and advocacy pressure. It runs a structured response and follow-through workflow across customer success, follow-up, marketing, and sales lanes.

### Level C

**Advanced trust and advocacy orchestration**

The system actively orchestrates reputation protection, review momentum, referral handling, advocacy tracking, and President-level visibility at a much deeper level.

---

## 3. Level A Definition

Level A means the company has a visible trust-and-advocacy record, but execution is still relatively simple and President-visible.

At Level A, Reviews & Referrals Director should:

- show open reviews clearly
- show which reviews still need response
- distinguish positive reviews from risky unresolved reviews
- show referral-ready customers
- show basic active referral sources
- show stalled or missing referral follow-through
- show basic reputation watch signals
- keep all trust pressure visible to the President
- connect review and referral records to the main customer record

Level A is the minimum launch standard for this director.

It creates truth first.

Without this truth, trust pressure stays invisible and advocacy stays wasted.

---

## 4. Level B Definition

Level B means the company has a connected reviews-and-referrals workflow instead of a passive visibility board.

At Level B, Reviews & Referrals Director should add:

- structured response workflow for open reviews
- clearer ownership of who responds and when
- stronger visibility of delayed review-response pressure
- structured referral-request workflow
- organized tracking of referral sources
- clearer handoff between Customer Success Director and Follow-Up Director
- stronger coordination with Marketing Director and Sales Director
- better escalation logic for reputation risk
- stronger visibility of advocacy performance across customers and sources

Level B should make review and referral handling operational, not just observable.

---

## 5. Level C Definition

Level C means the company has advanced trust-and-advocacy orchestration across review protection, customer advocacy, referral flow, and President-level control.

At Level C, Reviews & Referrals Director should add:

- advanced review follow-through orchestration
- stronger automated prompting and reminder logic
- richer reputation-risk categorization
- stronger referral follow-through automation
- deeper visibility into advocacy quality and referral strength
- stronger coordination with Marketing, Sales, Customer Success, and President strategy lanes
- clearer trust-health performance visibility over time
- stronger executive visibility into where public trust is growing or weakening

Level C should feel like a real operating system for public trust and customer advocacy.

---

## 6. Exact Launch Difference Between A, B, And C

### A vs B

Level A shows trust and advocacy truth.

Level B adds structured workflow, ownership, and connected follow-through.

### B vs C

Level B runs connected workflow.

Level C adds deeper orchestration, stronger automated momentum, richer risk handling, and stronger executive-level trust management.

### Plain-English Lock

- A = we can see trust and advocacy pressure
- B = we can manage trust and advocacy pressure through workflow
- C = the system orchestrates trust and advocacy as a strategic operating lane

---

## 7. Dependency Guidance

Reviews & Referrals Director is highly connected, but it can still exist as a commercially understandable lane by itself if the business mainly wants reputation visibility and referral structure.

### Can function at Level A with light dependency

Yes, if the business mainly needs:

- review visibility
- response discipline visibility
- referral-ready customer visibility
- basic advocacy tracking

### Becomes much stronger with these connected directors

- **Customer Success Director**
  Provides the customer-health context behind review and advocacy signals.
- **Follow-Up Director**
  Handles unresolved customer items that may affect reviews and referrals.
- **Marketing Director**
  Helps turn positive sentiment into reputation growth and advocacy use.
- **Sales Director**
  Helps convert referrals and visible trust into revenue.
- **Service Director / COO-side lanes**
  Supplies execution truth that often creates review outcomes.
- **Call Handling Director**
  Helps preserve public trust when communication quality drives customer sentiment.

### Recommended dependency logic

- Level A can be sold with light dependencies
- Level B is much stronger with Customer Success and Follow-Up
- Level C should usually be recommended with Customer Success, Follow-Up, and at least one growth lane such as Marketing or Sales

---

## 8. Recommendation Logic Guidance

Recommend Reviews & Referrals Director when the company says things like:

- “Reviews matter a lot in our market.”
- “We don’t always respond to reviews on time.”
- “We get referrals, but it’s informal.”
- “We want more repeat trust and word-of-mouth.”
- “Bad reviews or unanswered reviews create pressure.”
- “We want a stronger public reputation.”

### Recommend Level A when

- the business needs visibility first
- review handling is loose or informal
- referral opportunity exists but is not managed
- the owner wants clearer trust truth before adding deeper systems

### Recommend Level B when

- the business already has meaningful customer volume
- review response needs structure
- referrals should be requested and tracked more consistently
- trust issues affect growth
- customer success and follow-up are already important lanes

### Recommend Level C when

- public trust strongly affects revenue
- the business wants advocacy as a deliberate growth engine
- referrals should be treated as a managed operating lane
- the company needs deeper coordination between trust, follow-up, and growth

---

## 9. Business-Friendly Explanation For Customers

Reviews & Referrals Director helps your company protect public trust and turn happy customers into visible growth opportunity.

At Level A, it shows where review pressure exists, which customers are ready to advocate for you, and where trust is at risk.

At Level B, it gives the business a real workflow for responding to reviews, requesting referrals, and keeping that work organized.

At Level C, it becomes a stronger orchestration layer that helps the company protect reputation, grow customer advocacy, and keep the President fully aware of where trust is helping or hurting growth.

---

## 10. What Must Be Avoided

Do not describe this director as only:

- a review widget
- a ratings page
- a simple referral form
- a marketing-only feature

That would undersell the real function.

The correct launch meaning is:

**Reviews & Referrals Director is the trust-and-advocacy operating lane that keeps public reputation visible and converts customer satisfaction into future growth.**

Also avoid:

- collapsing reviews into Customer Success completely
- collapsing referrals into Marketing completely
- treating advocacy as invisible after-service noise

This director exists because trust and advocacy need their own visible command lane.
