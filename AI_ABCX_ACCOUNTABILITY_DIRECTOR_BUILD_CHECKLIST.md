## 1. Accountability Director Mission

Accountability Director is the follow-through and operating-discipline lane of AI-ABCX.

Its job is to:

- make ownership visible
- show where commitments are slipping
- track whether required actions were actually completed
- reduce ambiguity around who owns what next
- surface quiet drift before it becomes a larger problem
- support escalation when follow-through is weak
- preserve visible operational discipline across the company
- connect goals, actions, and reporting into one accountable system

Accountability Director should not feel like a vague “score” feature.

It should feel like the part of the company system that prevents tasks, promises, approvals, and commitments from disappearing into noise.

---

## 2. Accountability Director Launch Standard

Accountability Director is launch-ready only when it can do all of the following:

1. assign visible ownership to required actions
2. show commitment status clearly
3. identify follow-through gaps
4. surface missing updates and late signals
5. distinguish active accountability from quiet drift
6. support escalation logic when owners do not follow through
7. connect cleanly with Executive Reporting, Strategy, Approval, Finance, People, and department-level workflows
8. support A / B / C levels clearly

If one of these is missing, the director is not complete.

---

## 3. Canonical Role Definition

### Public-facing name

`Accountability Director`

### Internal role meaning

The AI-ABCX director responsible for making commitments, ownership, follow-through, updates, and escalation visible so the President can govern execution instead of guessing where responsibility is slipping.

### Executive owner

`CEO`

Accountability Director belongs to the CEO lane because it governs company-wide operating discipline, ownership clarity, and follow-through quality across every active director.

## 7. A / B / C Definition Draft

### Level A

Level A Accountability Director is visible ownership and commitment discipline.

Includes:

- visible action ownership
- commitment status
- missing update visibility
- basic accountability gap detection
- simple escalation readiness

Best fit:

- smaller service businesses
- owner-led teams
- companies needing better follow-through before deeper automation

### Level B

Level B Accountability Director adds stronger coordination and review discipline.

Includes everything in A, plus:

- better accountability summaries
- stronger escalation pathways
- stronger accountability dependencies across departments
- better periodic review structure
- better connection to reporting quality

Best fit:

- growing businesses
- multi-director configurations
- companies with more handoffs and more than one layer of execution

### Level C

Level C Accountability Director adds advanced operating-governance automation.

Includes everything in B, plus:

- stronger automated accountability logic
- better drift detection
- richer cross-lane follow-through analysis
- stronger preparation for people-performance management
- stronger President-level accountability governance

Best fit:

- larger or scaling service companies
- businesses using many active directors
- companies that need stricter operating discipline without adding more human management layers

That record should preserve:

## 13. Required Handoffs

Accountability Director does not work alone.

It must hand off cleanly to and from:

- Executive Reporting Director
- Strategy Director
- Approval Director
- Revenue Control Director
- Reconciliation Director
- Scheduling Director
- Service Director
- Support Director
- Retention Director
- People / later people automation logic

Examples:

- Executive Reporting gives Accountability the summary and decision context
- Accountability gives Executive Reporting the true follow-through condition
- Approval gives Accountability unresolved ownership and blocked commitments
- Finance lanes give Accountability financial confirmation and correction gaps
- Scheduling and Service give Accountability operational follow-through states
- People surfaces later consume accountability visibility as groundwork for stronger personnel governance

---

## 14. Guided Activation Requirements

Guided activation must determine:

- how the business currently tracks follow-through
- whether ownership is clear or informal
- whether missing updates are common
- whether commitments are being lost between office, field, and owner
- whether escalation is currently structured or improvised
- whether the owner wants light visibility or stronger operating discipline

The system should use this to recommend:

- whether Accountability Director is needed only at A
- whether a stronger B configuration is justified
- whether a C-level operating-discipline lane is appropriate

---

## 15. Pricing Decisions Still Required

The following pricing questions are still unresolved:

- whether Accountability Director is always included as a core CEO lane or priced separately
- if priced separately, whether it should be treated as a premium discipline layer
- whether B and C accountability upgrades are tied to broader reporting or people-governance upgrades
- whether accountability logic should be packaged more strongly in improved / advanced recommendations

Until that is decided, build should proceed independently of pricing finalization.

---

## 16. Build Sequence

Recommended build order:

1. normalize Accountability Director role definition
2. normalize its A / B / C scope
3. define accountability categories and states
4. define shared-record fields for ownership, follow-through, and escalation
5. normalize Accountability Director dashboard surfaces
6. connect it to Executive Reporting
7. connect it to Approval and Finance dependencies
8. connect it to People-accountability bridge logic
9. define monthly accountability review behavior
10. test gap detection, ambiguity detection, and escalation states

This order matters because accountability without shared operating truth quickly becomes fake governance.

1. ownership assignment remains visible across updates
