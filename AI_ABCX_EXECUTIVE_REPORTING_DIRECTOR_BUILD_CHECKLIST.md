## 1. Executive Reporting Director Mission

Executive Reporting Director is the board-briefing and executive signal-consolidation lane of AI-ABCX.

Its job is to:

- consolidate the strongest company signals into a short executive narrative
- reduce dashboard complexity into action-ready summaries
- highlight decision load clearly for the President
- preserve signal quality across departments
- distinguish important changes from noise
- keep daily and periodic reporting tied to decisions, not just metrics
- make the board-facing view readable, brief, and trustworthy

Executive Reporting Director should not feel like a static report generator.

It should feel like the company’s executive briefing layer under President control.

---

## 2. Executive Reporting Director Launch Standard

Executive Reporting Director is launch-ready only when it can do all of the following:

1. produce short executive summaries from shared operating truth
2. surface the strongest signal clearly
3. show decision load without creating noise
4. preserve reporting quality and summary discipline
5. support daily, weekly, and monthly reporting structures
6. coordinate cleanly with Strategy Director, Accountability Director, CEO view, and all department lanes
7. make President decision-making faster rather than heavier
8. support A / B / C levels clearly

If one of these is missing, the director is not complete.

`Executive Reporting Director`

The AI-ABCX director responsible for consolidating company signals into board-facing summaries, highlighting the sharpest current signal, framing decision load, and preserving executive reporting clarity across departments.

### Executive owner

`CEO`

Executive Reporting Director belongs to the CEO lane because it sits at the top of executive interpretation and turns cross-company operating truth into a President-readable brief.

Executive Reporting Director must match the practical expectations customers already have from strong reporting systems:

- summary readability
- decision-oriented reporting
- metric visibility without overload
- consistency of daily reporting
- clarity of what changed
- executive-ready presentation

At minimum, it must not feel weaker than a strong executive dashboard summary or management briefing layer.

Executive Reporting Director can beat typical outside reporting tools because:

- it sits inside one President-led corporate system
- it reads from the same shared truth as Sales, Service, Scheduling, Support, Finance, and Marketing
- it can compress all departments into one executive language instead of disconnected dashboards
- it can tie every brief directly to next recommended actions

## 7. A / B / C Definition Draft

### Level A

Level A Executive Reporting Director is concise executive signal visibility.

Includes:

- daily executive summary
- current strongest signal
- visible decision load
- short board-ready narrative
- basic cross-lane summary discipline

Best fit:

- smaller businesses
- President-led companies
- companies needing clarity more than deep reporting complexity

### Level B

Level B Executive Reporting Director adds stronger summary intelligence.

Includes everything in A, plus:

- better department prioritization
- stronger periodic reporting
- clearer trend framing
- better signal weighting
- stronger coordination with Accountability and Strategy

Best fit:

- growing teams
- multi-director configurations
- companies that need more management rhythm

### Level C

Level C Executive Reporting Director adds advanced board-level orchestration.

Includes everything in B, plus:

- stronger automated narrative generation
- deeper signal ranking
- richer cross-department synthesis
- stronger monthly and executive-cycle reporting
- more autonomous recommendation framing for the President

Best fit:

- larger or scaling service businesses
- businesses operating many active directors
- companies needing higher management discipline without a large internal executive layer

## 9. Required Reporting States

At minimum, Executive Reporting Director should support:

- `summary_ready`
- `signal_ranked`
- `decision_visible`
- `brief_needs_refresh`
- `department_input_missing`
- `noise_reduced`
- `board_ready`
- `president_attention_needed`

These states should keep reporting governed and trustworthy rather than ad hoc.

## 10. Required Shared Record

Executive Reporting Director must operate from the same company truth used by the rest of AI-ABCX.

That record should preserve:

- department source
- signal timestamp
- summary category
- signal priority
- decision impact
- related owner
- related next action
- board score context
- report freshness
- President-facing narrative

Executive Reporting Director should never create a detached reporting truth layer.

- Executive Summary

- strongest-signal summary

## 12. Required Dashboard Visibility

Executive Reporting Director must be visible in the right places:

- President Dashboard
- CEO lane view

- board score / executive summary areas
- launch configuration and activation logic

The President should be able to understand:

- what the sharpest signal is
- what the decision load is
- whether the brief is fresh and usable
- whether reporting quality is strong enough to trust

---

## 13. Required Handoffs

Executive Reporting Director must hand off cleanly to:

- `Strategy Director`
  - when current reporting implies a strategic adjustment
- `Accountability Director`
  - when a summary exposes owner drift or unresolved commitments
- `Revenue Control Director`
  - when finance becomes the primary signal
- `Sales Director`
  - when conversion pressure becomes the dominant executive concern
- `Service Director`
  - when execution pressure becomes the dominant executive concern

Weak handoffs would make the briefing layer decorative instead of operational.

---

## 14. Guided Activation Requirements

The system must be able to determine whether Executive Reporting Director should be included by asking human questions such as:

- Do you want a daily executive summary of what matters most?
- Do you feel like your business data is scattered across too many places?
- Do you want the system to tell you what needs your attention first?
- Do you want monthly goals and executive reporting to be visible, not improvised?
- Do you want one President-level brief instead of checking several dashboards?

Activation should determine:

- whether the customer needs simple or advanced executive reporting
- whether Accountability Director should be bundled
- whether the company is ready for A, B, or C
- whether the customer wants board-style reporting rhythm

## 15. Pricing Decisions Still Required

This checklist does not lock pricing.

But pricing architecture must eventually decide:

- whether Executive Reporting Director is always bundled as part of the core management layer
- whether it can be priced separately
- whether A is considered included and B/C are upgrades
- whether monthly / board-style reporting depth changes price materially
- how it should appear in partial, improved, and advanced recommended configurations

## 16. Build Sequence

Recommended implementation order:

1. normalize canonical Executive Reporting Director identity across launch planning
2. define summary categories and state model
3. define strongest-signal and decision-load logic
4. normalize reporting tabs into one director surface
5. connect the surface to the shared company record
6. connect handoffs into Strategy, Accountability, and department directors
7. define A / B / C rules
8. connect President-facing reporting outputs
9. test reporting flows end to end

This sequence keeps the briefing layer useful before making it too elaborate.

---

## 17. Internal Tests Required

At minimum, Executive Reporting Director should be tested for:

1. daily summary generated from real shared company signals
2. strongest signal surfaced correctly when finance becomes primary
3. decision load shown without noise inflation
4. stale or missing department inputs flagged correctly
5. Accountability and Strategy handoffs created correctly
6. board-ready summary remains concise and readable
7. President view reflecting current executive pressure accurately

If these tests fail, the director is not ready.
