# AI-ABCX Support Director A/B/C Launch Definition

## 1. Core Rule

Support Director is the customer-response and issue-resolution lane of AI-ABCX.

Its job is to control:

- open support visibility
- response ownership
- delayed-reply visibility
- escalation visibility
- issue continuity
- support history preservation
- visible follow-through on customer problems

It is not the same as:

- Customer Success Director, which protects broader relationship continuity
- Follow-Up Director, which manages unresolved next-step discipline
- Service Director, which manages execution truth after work is assigned
- Call Handling Director, which manages live AI conversation intake and handling

Support Director controls what happens after a customer problem, question, or friction point already exists.

---

## 2. Public Meaning Of Each Level

### Level A
Visible support truth.

### Level B
Connected support workflow.

### Level C
Advanced support orchestration.

---

## 3. Level A Definition

Level A Support Director means the business can clearly see:

- which support items are open
- who owns each issue
- which replies are delayed
- which issues are waiting on action
- which customer threads carry visible risk
- what support pressure exists right now

Level A is a visibility-and-discipline layer first.

At Level A:

- open support items do not disappear
- response ownership is visible
- delayed replies are visible
- customer issues remain tied to real records
- the President can see active support pressure

Level A is the minimum level required for real support control.

---

## 4. Level B Definition

Level B Support Director means support becomes more coordinated, not just visible.

At Level B:

- support ownership is stronger
- escalation routing is clearer
- support issues connect more cleanly to adjacent lanes
- context and history are preserved more consistently
- unresolved issues move through stronger response discipline
- support work becomes easier to manage as an operating workflow

Level B turns support from visible issue handling into a connected response workflow.

This is the level where the business starts feeling that customer problems are being actively managed, not simply tracked.

---

## 5. Level C Definition

Level C Support Director means support becomes a high-control issue-handling lane.

At Level C:

- escalation risk is surfaced earlier
- support intervention logic is stronger
- issue handling becomes more structured
- customer-friction patterns are more visible
- cross-lane coordination is deeper
- executive visibility into support health is stronger

Level C is for businesses that want a more advanced support-control layer with stronger issue discipline and earlier risk visibility.

This is the most complete version of support orchestration at launch.

---

## 6. Exact Launch Difference Between A, B, And C

### A = visible support truth

The company can clearly see:

- open issues
- delayed replies
- who owns the issue
- what still needs response
- whether support pressure is rising

### B = connected support workflow

Everything in A, plus:

- stronger response discipline
- stronger escalation routing
- stronger issue continuity
- stronger context preservation
- stronger handoff coordination

### C = advanced support orchestration

Everything in B, plus:

- stronger risk detection
- stronger intervention logic
- stronger support-health visibility
- stronger cross-lane customer-friction control
- stronger executive control over unresolved support pressure

---

## 7. Dependency Guidance

Support Director can exist with partial independence, but it becomes much stronger when paired with adjacent customer-continuity lanes.

### Can Support Director exist by itself?

Yes, in a limited form.

This makes sense when a company already has:

- active customers
- recurring questions or problems
- scattered customer-response activity
- a need for visible issue ownership first

### Strongest pairings

Support Director becomes much stronger with:

- Customer Success Director
- Follow-Up Director
- Call Handling Director
- Service Director
- Scheduling Director
- Dispatch Director

### Practical rule

- Support A can stand alone more credibly
- Support B usually works best with at least one adjacent director
- Support C works best as part of a broader customer-continuity system

---

## 8. Recommendation Logic Guidance

Recommend **Support Director A** when:

- customer issues sometimes get lost
- response ownership is weak
- customers have to repeat the same problem
- leadership needs a clean view of open support work

Recommend **Support Director B** when:

- support work needs stronger coordination
- escalation routing is inconsistent
- service, follow-up, and customer history are not connecting well
- response discipline needs improvement

Recommend **Support Director C** when:

- the company wants a higher-control support lane
- customer-friction patterns matter
- escalation prevention matters
- support is becoming operationally costly
- customer-response health needs deeper executive visibility

---

## 9. Business-Friendly Explanation For Customers

### Support Director A

You can clearly see what customer issues are open, who owns them, which replies are late, and where support pressure is building.

### Support Director B

Your support lane becomes more organized and coordinated, with stronger ownership, escalation handling, and issue continuity.

### Support Director C

Your support lane becomes a higher-control system with stronger risk visibility, better intervention logic, and deeper coordination across the business.

---

## 10. What Must Be Avoided

Do not describe Support Director as:

- just a message inbox
- only customer service chat
- only complaint tracking
- only a ticket board

That makes it too small.

Support Director is the operating truth of customer-response discipline once an issue already exists.

Also avoid pretending:

- A is heavily intelligent
- B is the same as C
- C is only “more reporting”

The level differences must stay operationally meaningful.

## 11. Pricing Use

- A = visibility and ownership truth
- B = workflow coordination and escalation discipline
- C = advanced support orchestration

That makes pricing easier to explain because the customer is paying for a stronger response-control lane, not random support features.
