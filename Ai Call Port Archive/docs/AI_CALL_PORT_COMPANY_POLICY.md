# AI Call Port Company Policy

This document defines the high-level operating policy for AI Call Port as a company and product system.

It is meant to keep the launch structure, customer model, partner model, and operational rules aligned.

## Relationship Principle

AI Call Port is not trying to build only a customer base.
It is trying to build a customer base and a fan base at the same time.

That means the company should operate in a way that makes customers and partners feel:

- involved
- heard
- respected
- rewarded when the system grows
- able to benefit from helping the system improve

This principle should influence:

- public messaging
- partner policy
- launch updates
- dashboard visibility
- referral structure
- advertising tone

The goal is not hype.
The goal is long-term trust, shared upside, and real participation.

## Company Model

AI Call Port is a modular business operating system.

The AI Call Agent is the foundation module and the first public launch module.

Customers can:

- use only the AI Call Agent
- add more modules later if they are useful
- rely on AI Call Port to help determine which modules fit their business

This means AI Call Port is not:

- only an AI receptionist
- or an all-or-nothing software suite

It is a modular system with guided configuration and expandable operational depth.

## Public Launch Policy

The first public launch is Stage A: the AI Call Agent.

Stage A is a real public product, not only a closed beta.

At this stage, AI Call Port should focus on:

- advanced AI call handling
- conversation-category logic
- lead capture
- qualification
- routing / escalation
- after-hours logic
- emergency logic
- business-specific handling quality

At this stage, AI Call Port should not depend on direct sales or service scheduling as part of the core offer.

The public launch should also be transparent.

That means:

- launch stages should be communicated clearly
- release dates should be communicated clearly
- testing updates should be shared publicly
- timeline adjustments should be explained openly with updated dates

Transparency should be treated as part of the launch model, not as an optional marketing tactic.

## Module Configuration Policy

Customers should not be expected to guess which modules they need.

AI Call Port includes an analysis and configuration layer that should determine:

- which modules are necessary
- which modules are optional
- which conversation logic categories are required
- which routing and fallback logic should be active
- which upgrade path makes sense over time

This is part of the product model, not only an informal consulting activity.

## Customer Plan Policy

AI Call Port should support at least two customer tracks for Stage A:

- Regular plan
- Partner plan

The regular plan is for customers who want the Stage A product without deeper participation requirements.

The partner plan is for customers who receive additional benefits in exchange for more structured participation.

## Partner Plan Benefits

Partner companies receive:

- reduced monthly pricing
- priority onboarding and setup attention
- tighter founder/operator review loop
- earlier visibility into improvements that affect their use case
- priority consideration for future module access when relevant
- stronger collaboration around script quality, routing quality, and business-fit logic
- a personal referral link tied to their partner account
- a `$15` monthly credit for each referred active customer
- dashboard visibility into referral activity and earned monthly credits

These benefits should be treated as real policy commitments, not casual promises.

## Partner Plan Responsibilities

Partner companies are expected to:

- provide consistent feedback on real system usage
- respond to follow-up questions when issues or patterns appear
- participate in periodic review or check-in conversations
- report failures, awkward moments, edge cases, and business-specific friction
- help confirm what is working well, not only what is failing
- participate in review, testimonial, quote, or case-study requests when the system is delivering real value

These responsibilities are part of the price difference.
The partner plan is not only a discount.

## Partner Referral Credit Policy

Each partner company should receive a personal referral link.

For every referred customer that becomes active, the partner should receive a `$15` credit against its monthly payment.

This means:

- referral credits are earned only for active referred customers
- the credit reduces the partner's monthly payment
- referral status and monthly credit totals should be visible in the partner dashboard

The referral-credit rules should be clearly defined in billing and dashboard logic before launch.

## Partner Proof Policy

Partner companies are expected to cooperate reasonably in producing public proof when results justify it.

This can include:

- written reviews
- short testimonials
- outcome-based quotes
- lightweight case studies
- permission to reference results appropriately

The goal is not forced praise.
The goal is credible public proof tied to real value.

## Partner Agreement Policy

A company should not receive partner pricing or partner benefits unless it explicitly agrees to the partner plan policy during the activation pipeline.

This agreement should confirm understanding of:

- partner pricing
- partner benefits
- feedback responsibilities
- review and proof expectations
- conditions for losing partner status

## Approval And Removal Policy

Not every company should automatically qualify for the partner plan.

Partner approval should be based on:

- business fit
- expected learning value
- willingness to participate
- clarity of use case
- likelihood of producing useful feedback and proof

A company can lose partner-plan status if it repeatedly fails to meet the responsibilities of the plan.

If that happens, AI Call Port may:

- move the company to the regular plan
- end partner-only benefits
- continue service under the normal plan if the customer still wants to proceed

## Internal Operating Rule

AI Call Port should treat these policies as structured company rules, not random one-off founder decisions.

That means:

- pricing distinctions should be justified
- partner obligations should be explained before acceptance
- module recommendations should follow the analysis/configuration logic
- activation should enforce the required agreements
- public messaging should match the actual operating structure
- transparency should be visible in launch communication and updates
- customer and partner participation should be treated as a strategic asset
