# AI-ABCX Follow-Up Director A B C Launch Definition

## 1. Core Rule

Follow-Up Director is not a basic reminder tool.

It is the AI-ABCX continuity lane responsible for keeping unresolved business promises visible, owned, and actionable under President control.

Its job is to make sure the company knows:

- what is still open
- why it is still open
- who owns the next step
- what should happen next
- what is overdue
- what must be escalated
- what has been resolved

At launch, Follow-Up Director must use one clean level structure:

- `Level A` = visible unresolved follow-up truth
- `Level B` = connected follow-up workflow
- `Level C` = advanced follow-up orchestration

---

## 2. Public Meaning Of Each Level

### Follow-Up Director Level A

Your company gets one clear follow-up record for unresolved estimates, scheduling items, service outcomes, and pending next steps, visible to the President.

### Follow-Up Director Level B

Your company gets a connected follow-up workflow with stronger reminder logic, ownership handling, and escalation discipline across unresolved business activity.

### Follow-Up Director Level C

Your company gets the strongest launch level of follow-up control, designed for multi-lane continuity, richer sequencing, stronger escalation structure, and tighter executive accountability.

---

## 3. Follow-Up Director Level A Definition

Level A is the foundation continuity layer.

It is built for companies that need unresolved business pressure to become visible before they need deeper orchestration.

### Level A must include

- follow-up record creation
- visible unresolved-item tracking
- estimate follow-up visibility
- scheduling follow-up visibility
- service-result follow-up visibility
- payment or revenue-truth follow-up visibility
- customer-response-needed visibility
- internal-owner-needed visibility
- visible next-step tracking
- due and overdue visibility
- President-visible unresolved pressure

### Level A business meaning

Level A says:

“Nothing important disappears after first contact, quote, booking, or service activity.”

### Level A is for companies that

- lose opportunities after the first interaction
- rely on memory for callbacks and pending actions
- need to see unfinished business clearly
- want follow-up discipline before deeper automation
- need owner visibility into what is still unresolved

### Level A does not require

- complex branching logic
- high-depth automated sequencing
- advanced multi-lane escalation structure
- richer orchestration across several operating lanes

### Level A promise

The company stops losing value in silence.

Unfinished business becomes visible and owned.

---

## 4. Follow-Up Director Level B Definition

Level B is the connected follow-up workflow layer.

It is built for companies that need more than visibility. They need the system to help actively move unresolved items toward resolution.

### Level B must include everything in Level A, plus

- stronger reminder structure
- more organized next-step ownership
- clearer escalation handling
- stronger continuity between estimate, schedule, and service states
- better separation between pending customer response and pending internal action
- stronger operating discipline around open opportunities
- tighter coordination with estimate-to-close and appointment-to-outcome movement
- stronger follow-up guidance about what should happen next

### Level B business meaning

Level B says:

“Your company is no longer just seeing unresolved work. It is actively controlling how unresolved work gets finished.”

### Level B is for companies that

- have several unresolved items moving at once
- need stronger discipline after quotes and bookings
- lose value because ownership is unclear
- need more structured reminder and escalation handling
- want continuity to feel like an operating lane, not a note system

### Level B promise

Follow-up becomes a working control system, not just a visibility screen.

---

## 5. Follow-Up Director Level C Definition

Level C is the advanced orchestration layer.

It is built for businesses that need the strongest launch level of follow-up control across multiple business realities.

### Level C must include everything in Level B, plus

- structured multi-lane follow-up orchestration
- stronger automated next-step sequencing
- richer escalation handling
- richer exception handling
- stronger executive accountability visibility
- deeper coordination across revenue, scheduling, service, and retention-adjacent continuity
- stronger control over unresolved pressure before it becomes leakage
- stronger prioritization across different follow-up categories

### Level C business meaning

Level C says:

“Follow-up is now a high-discipline operating control system across the company, not a supporting reminder layer.”

### Level C is for companies that

- run more complexity
- need stronger continuity across several lanes
- want deeper operational discipline
- want unresolved pressure managed before it damages revenue or service quality
- are ready for a more automated operating structure

### Level C promise

Follow-up becomes one of the strongest recovery and accountability systems in the business.

---

## 7. Dependency Guidance

Follow-Up Director can be sold independently.

It does not require another director in order to create value.

### Follow-Up Director works independently when the goal is

- stopping silent opportunity loss
- making unresolved items visible
- creating ownership and next-step discipline
- recovering missed estimate or booking follow-up
- giving the President clarity about unfinished business

### Follow-Up Director becomes stronger with

- `Estimator Director`
- `Call Handling Director`
- `Scheduling Director`
- `Sales Director`
- `Service Director`
- `Revenue Control Director`

### Strong launch combinations

- `Estimator + Follow-Up`
- `Call Handling + Follow-Up`
- `Scheduling + Follow-Up`
- `Estimator + Scheduling + Follow-Up`
- `Call Handling + Estimator + Scheduling + Follow-Up`

### Important rule

Follow-Up Director must not be described as “only supporting automation.”

It is commercially independent.

But it becomes more powerful when combined with the directors that create unresolved pressure in the first place.

---

## 8. Recommendation Logic Guidance

Recommend `Level A` when:

- the company loses track of pending next steps
- callbacks, estimate chases, or unresolved items live in memory
- the biggest need is visibility and follow-up discipline first

Recommend `Level B` when:

- more than one person touches unresolved work
- the business needs stronger reminder and escalation logic
- continuity between quote, schedule, and service is getting harder to manage

Recommend `Level C` when:

- unresolved pressure crosses several operating lanes
- follow-up discipline must be stronger at scale
- the owner wants tighter control over missed or pending business value
- executive visibility and escalation depth matter more

---

## 9. Business-Friendly Explanation For Customers

Follow-Up Director controls what happens after something important starts but has not finished yet.

At Level A, it makes unfinished business visible.

At Level B, it adds stronger reminder, ownership, and escalation workflow.

At Level C, it becomes a higher-control continuity system that helps manage unresolved pressure across the company.

## 10. What Must Be Avoided

Do not describe:

- `A` as just a reminder list
- `B` as just “more reminders”
- `C` as generic automation without business meaning

Do not confuse:

- follow-up continuity
- sales pipeline stages
- scheduling states
- service execution states

Follow-Up Director owns unresolved next-step continuity.

It coordinates with other lanes, but its identity must remain clear.
