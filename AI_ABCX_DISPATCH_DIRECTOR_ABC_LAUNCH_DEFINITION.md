# AI-ABCX Dispatch Director A B C Launch Definition

## 1. Core Rule

Dispatch Director is not a generic dispatch board.

It is the AI-ABCX live movement lane responsible for controlling assignment movement after scheduling truth already exists.

Its job is to make sure the company knows:

- what is waiting for assignment
- who owns each live job
- what is blocked
- what needs reroute
- what is in progress
- what is closeout-pending
- what pressure is building across the day

At launch, Dispatch Director must use one clear level structure:

- `Level A` = visible manual dispatch truth
- `Level B` = connected dispatch workflow
- `Level C` = advanced dispatch orchestration

---

## 2. Public Meaning Of Each Level

### Dispatch Director Level A

Your company gets one clear assignment view with visible worker or crew ownership, visible blockers, and President-level operational movement truth.

### Dispatch Director Level B

Your company gets a connected dispatch workflow with stronger confirmation handling, reroute support, urgency visibility, and cleaner day-control discipline.

### Dispatch Director Level C

Your company gets the strongest launch level of dispatch control, designed for higher pressure, stronger coordination, richer prioritization, and more advanced movement handling across the service day.

---

## 3. Dispatch Director Level A Definition

Level A is the foundation dispatch visibility layer.

It is built for companies that still make assignment decisions manually but need those decisions to stay visible, structured, and connected to the same job record.

### Level A must include

- visible assignment queue
- manual assignment control
- worker or crew ownership visibility
- dispatch blocker visibility
- confirmation status visibility
- reroute visibility
- in-progress visibility
- closeout-pending visibility
- President-visible operational pressure
- one clean movement record connected to the same shared job truth

### Level A business meaning

Level A says:

“Your dispatch is still human-led where needed, but it is no longer invisible, improvised, or easy to lose.”

### Level A is for companies that

- have workers or crews in the field
- already schedule work but lose control during live movement
- need assignment visibility before deeper automation
- need same-day operational clarity
- want the President and COO to see live movement without chaos

### Level A does not require

- advanced routing automation
- deeper balancing logic
- richer prioritization engines
- higher orchestration depth

### Level A promise

The company stops losing control between confirmed appointment and field execution.

---

## 4. Dispatch Director Level B Definition

Level B is the connected dispatch workflow layer.

It is built for companies that need more than assignment visibility. They need stronger control over how field movement behaves across the day.

### Level B must include everything in Level A, plus

- worker confirmation logic
- stronger reroute handling
- urgency prioritization
- stronger assignment-state discipline
- cleaner sales, service, and combined-role movement handling
- stronger same-day operational pressure awareness
- better coordination between assignment, in-progress work, and next-step control
- stronger visibility into why the day is becoming operationally strained

### Level B business meaning

Level B says:

“Your company is no longer just seeing assignment movement. It is actively controlling the quality and discipline of the movement itself.”

### Level B is for companies that

- run several active workers or crews
- deal with same-day changes often
- need stronger assignment discipline
- lose time because reroutes and confirmations are messy
- want operations to feel controlled, not improvised

### Level B promise

Dispatch becomes a real operating workflow, not just a board that reflects yesterday’s confusion.

---

## 5. Dispatch Director Level C Definition

Level C is the advanced dispatch orchestration layer.

It is built for businesses that need the strongest launch level of live operational movement control.

### Level C must include everything in Level B, plus

- advanced routing logic
- stronger prioritization automation
- stronger balancing of assignment pressure
- richer movement-state coordination
- tighter accountability signals across dispatch movement
- stronger handling of higher-volume operational strain
- stronger operating recommendations when the day starts breaking down

### Level C business meaning

Level C says:

“Dispatch is now a high-control operating system across the field day, not just a human tracking surface.”

### Level C is for companies that

- manage more field complexity
- have several live movement lanes
- need stronger same-day control
- want tighter coordination between assignment, route pressure, and closeout flow
- are scaling and do not want dispatch chaos to become the bottleneck

### Level C promise

Dispatch becomes one of the strongest real-time control lanes in the company.

## 7. Dependency Guidance

Dispatch Director should not usually be sold as the first operational director for a business that has no scheduling truth.

It is commercially meaningful, but it is not the same type of independent starter module as Call Handling, Website, Estimator, or Follow-Up.

### Dispatch Director can work independently only when

- the company already has a real appointment and assignment flow
- the main problem is field movement after the booking already exists
- the business already has scheduling truth but weak operational assignment control

### Dispatch Director becomes strongest with

- `Scheduling Director`
- `Service Director`
- `Follow-Up Director`
- `Call Handling Director`
- `Revenue Control Director`

### Strong launch combinations

- `Scheduling + Dispatch`
- `Dispatch + Service`
- `Scheduling + Dispatch + Service`
- `Call Handling + Scheduling + Dispatch`
- `Scheduling + Dispatch + Follow-Up + Revenue Control`

### Important rule

Dispatch Director is not the first source of appointment truth.

It begins after the business already knows the work is real and ready for operational movement.

---

## 8. Recommendation Logic Guidance

Recommend `Level A` when:

- the business already assigns work manually
- live movement is confusing
- field ownership is unclear
- the company needs operational visibility before deeper dispatch automation

Recommend `Level B` when:

- several workers or crews are active
- same-day changes create pressure
- confirmations, reroutes, and assignment discipline need structure
- the owner wants cleaner day control

Recommend `Level C` when:

- field movement is more complex
- dispatch pressure hits multiple jobs and crews at once
- the company needs stronger balancing and prioritization
- scaling requires tighter live movement control

---

## 9. Business-Friendly Explanation For Customers

Dispatch Director controls what happens after the appointment is already real and the work has to move through the field correctly.

At Level A, it makes assignment movement visible and controlled.

At Level B, it adds stronger same-day workflow discipline.

At Level C, it becomes a higher-control dispatch system built for more pressure and more moving parts.

---

## 10. What Must Be Avoided

Do not describe:

- `A` as just a calendar
- `B` as just “more dispatch”
- `C` as generic automation with no business meaning

Do not confuse:

- scheduling truth
- dispatch movement
- service execution

Scheduling says the appointment is real.

Dispatch says who owns the work and how the live movement stays under control.
