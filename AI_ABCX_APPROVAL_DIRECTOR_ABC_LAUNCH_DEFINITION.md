# AI-ABCX Approval Director A/B/C Launch Definition

## 1. Core Rule
Approval Director controls how important business decisions stay visible, assigned, routed, and reviewable.

It is not just an approval button.

It is the decision-routing control lane that prevents:
- lost approvals
- unclear ownership
- silent delays
- scattered decision-making
- governance drift

At launch, Approval Director must help the company understand:
- what needs approval
- who owns the approval
- whether the approval is blocked
- whether the approval escalated
- whether the President needs to see it

---

## 2. Public Meaning Of Each Level

### Approval Director A
Visible approval routing.

### Approval Director B
Connected approval workflow.

### Approval Director C
Advanced approval orchestration.

These three levels should feel clear to a business owner:

- `A` = approvals are visible and assigned
- `B` = approvals move through a structured workflow
- `C` = approvals are deeply controlled, escalated, and connected to governance logic

---

## 3. Level A Definition
Approval Director A means AI-ABCX gives the company visible approval routing.

What must be true:
- pending approvals are visible
- approval owner is visible
- approval reason is visible
- escalation state is visible
- approval record stays attached to the business workflow
- President can see when decision attention is needed

What A does well:
- stops approvals from disappearing into texts, calls, memory, or informal handoffs
- creates basic routing discipline
- helps smaller companies move from verbal decision-making to visible decision-making

What A does not try to do:
- heavy approval automation
- advanced exception handling
- governance-grade approval policy orchestration

Plain meaning:
A means the business can finally see approvals clearly instead of chasing them informally.

---

## 4. Level B Definition
Approval Director B means AI-ABCX gives the company connected approval workflow.

Everything in A remains true, plus:
- approval categories are structured
- approval aging becomes visible
- escalation routing becomes stronger
- ownership enforcement becomes clearer
- approval flow becomes more standardized by type
- approval state connects more tightly to reporting and accountability

What B does well:
- reduces approval confusion in multi-person teams
- makes delayed decisions easier to spot
- creates stronger decision discipline across departments
- supports companies where approvals affect finance, operations, customer handling, or compliance

What B still does not fully do:
- deepest governance automation
- highest audit-style exception logic
- full governance-grade orchestration across all approval dependencies

Plain meaning:
B means the company no longer just sees approvals. It manages them through a clearer, repeatable workflow.

---

## 5. Level C Definition
Approval Director C means AI-ABCX gives the company advanced approval orchestration.

Everything in B remains true, plus:
- approval routing is more deeply automated
- exception handling is stronger
- escalation paths are more structured
- approval history becomes more complete
- governance visibility becomes more audit-ready
- approval logic connects more tightly to compliance, risk, accountability, and executive reporting

What C does well:
- supports larger or more sensitive decision environments
- reduces governance breakdown risk
- gives the President clearer control over high-impact approvals
- makes approvals part of controlled business infrastructure, not just team process

Plain meaning:
C means approvals operate as part of a high-discipline governance system.

---

## 6. Exact Launch Difference Between A, B, And C

### A
- visible pending approvals
- visible decision owner
- visible escalation status
- record-attached approval trail
- manual but explicit routing

### B
- everything in A
- structured approval categories
- approval aging visibility
- stronger routing enforcement
- stronger escalation tracking
- more connected workflow behavior

### C
- everything in B
- deeper routing automation
- stronger exception handling
- fuller approval history
- stronger governance readiness
- tighter integration with compliance, risk, accountability, and reporting

---

## 7. Dependency Guidance
Approval Director is usually strongest inside the broader CAO / governance lane.

### Can it exist at Level A by itself?
Yes, in a limited sense.

If a business simply needs:
- visible approvals
- explicit decision ownership
- basic escalation visibility

then `A` can function as a visible governance-routing layer.

### Does B depend on other directors?
Usually yes.

`B` becomes much stronger when connected to:
- Accountability Director
- Executive Reporting Director
- Risk Director

### Does C depend on other directors?
Yes, strongly.

`C` should usually be treated as part of a connected governance package with:
- Compliance Director
- Risk Director
- Accountability Director
- Executive Reporting Director

### Best dependency summary
- `A` can work as visible routing
- `B` works best with accountability and reporting
- `C` should usually be sold as part of deeper governance control

---

## 8. Recommendation Logic Guidance

### Recommend A when:
- the company still routes approvals informally
- the owner wants clearer decision visibility
- approvals are mostly manual but need to stop disappearing
- the business is small but starting to add structure

### Recommend B when:
- approvals happen across multiple people
- ownership confusion already causes delays
- the business needs stronger routing discipline
- executive reporting and accountability need approval visibility

### Recommend C when:
- the business has high governance sensitivity
- approval drift creates real operational or financial risk
- the company needs stronger escalation and exception control
- approvals must connect tightly to compliance, accountability, and reporting

---

## 9. Business-Friendly Explanation For Customers
Approval Director helps your company stop losing important decisions in text messages, memory, side conversations, and unclear handoffs.

It makes sure the business can see:
- what needs approval
- who owns the decision
- what is waiting
- what escalated
- when the President should step in

That matters because many companies do not actually lose control from lack of effort.

They lose control because approvals are informal, unclear, delayed, or scattered.

Approval Director makes that decision lane visible and structured.

---

## 10. What Must Be Avoided
Approval Director should not be described as:
- just a button to approve things
- a generic admin workflow tool
- a passive record field

It must not feel fake at launch.

So we should avoid promising:
- deep automation at A
- governance-grade orchestration at B
- full audit behavior unless C actually supports it

The customer promise has to remain honest:
- A = visible routing
- B = connected workflow
- C = advanced governance orchestration
