# AI-ABC Stage A Management Hierarchy

Bring Corporate Structure to Service Businesses.

## Purpose

This document defines the exact management hierarchy for `Stage A`.

`Stage A` is the communication and opportunity-capture foundation of `AI-ABC`, but it is not only a call layer. It is already a structured company-management environment with defined reporting lines, director ownership, executive oversight, and President visibility.

This hierarchy must remain stable as `Stage B` and `Stage C` are added later.

---

## Core Principle

At `Stage A`, the business is already operating through a real corporate structure.

Even though automatic scheduling and advanced management are not yet active, the system still needs:

- operational ownership
- revenue ownership
- financial-truth ownership
- director-level supervision
- executive synthesis
- President visibility

The President does not manage raw activity directly.

The President receives summarized reporting through the executive structure, while still retaining the ability to drill down into any executive report, director report, customer record, or activity trail.

---

## President

The `President` is the business owner.

Responsibilities:

- approve goals
- review executive summaries
- review critical escalations
- confirm financial outcomes
- approve important management decisions
- govern by priorities, approvals, and results

The President is not rated.

The President receives:

- leadership activity reporting
- approval reporting
- financial reconciliation reporting
- unresolved escalation reporting
- executive recommendation reporting

---

## CEO

The `CEO` is the executive synthesis layer.

The CEO does not replace the other executives. The CEO consolidates their reporting and presents the business to the President in one governing view.

Responsibilities:

- summarize company condition
- combine executive reports
- identify top risks
- identify top opportunities
- prepare daily, weekly, and monthly briefings
- recommend next President actions
- reduce complexity into one executive summary

The CEO reports directly to the President.

---

## COO

The `COO` owns operational execution at `Stage A`.

At this stage, operations includes communication performance, manual service workflow, and operational follow-through before full scheduling automation is added.

Responsibilities:

- inbound communication operations
- outbound communication operations
- SMS communication operations
- missed-call recovery operations
- communication response discipline
- manual service workflow visibility
- service follow-through reporting
- operational exception visibility

The COO answers:

- Did communication happen correctly?
- Did the system and people respond correctly?
- Did service-side follow-through happen?

The COO reports to the CEO.

---

## CRO

The `CRO` owns revenue opportunity movement at `Stage A`.

The CRO does not own raw communication execution. The CRO owns what communication creates in revenue terms.

Responsibilities:

- qualified opportunity visibility
- lead movement
- quote and estimate opportunity progression
- conversion visibility
- stalled opportunity review
- revenue-at-risk visibility
- reactivation opportunity oversight
- referral opportunity oversight

The CRO answers:

- Which communication became opportunity?
- Which opportunity became estimate?
- Which opportunity stalled or was lost?
- Where is revenue forming or leaking?

The CRO reports to the CEO.

---

## CFO

The `CFO` owns financial truth at `Stage A`.

At Stage A, this is still a lighter layer than later stages, but it is already critical.

Responsibilities:

- expected estimate visibility
- owner-entered amount received
- financial reconciliation discipline
- revenue pending visibility
- revenue received visibility
- open financial-outcome confirmation

The CFO answers:

- What revenue is expected?
- What revenue was actually received?
- Which completed jobs still have no confirmed outcome?

The CFO reports to the CEO.

---

## Director of Communications

The `Director of Communications` reports to the `COO`.

This role supervises the quality and effectiveness of the communication layer.

Responsibilities:

- AI call agent performance
- inbound call quality
- outbound call quality
- SMS campaign quality
- missed-call recovery quality
- follow-up quality
- communication workflow consistency
- communication-level issue detection

The Director of Communications does not replace the AI call agent. This role manages communication performance as an operating function.

The Director of Communications answers:

- Is the communication layer working well?
- Are inbound and outbound workflows being handled correctly?
- Is communication quality creating or losing opportunity?

---

## Service Director

The `Service Director` reports to the `COO`.

At Stage A, the Service Director manages the manual service execution layer before automatic scheduling is introduced.

Responsibilities:

- manual service assignment visibility
- customer-number based service handoff
- service confirmation discipline
- service completion reporting
- field comment capture
- service follow-through visibility

The Service Director answers:

- Were manually assigned service jobs acknowledged?
- Were service jobs completed?
- Was completion reported correctly into the system?

---

## Sales Director

The `Sales Director` reports to the `CRO`.

At Stage A, the Sales Director manages manually routed sales opportunities before automatic scheduling is introduced.

Responsibilities:

- assigned lead follow-up discipline
- customer-number based sales handoff
- appointment confirmation by phone
- estimate amount entry
- lead status progression
- quote follow-up visibility
- salesperson responsiveness

The Sales Director answers:

- Were qualified opportunities followed up?
- Were estimate amounts captured?
- Did salespeople move leads correctly through the revenue process?

---

## AI Call Handling Officer

The `AI Call Handling Officer` reports to the `Director of Communications`.

This role is the direct operating agent of the Stage A communication layer.

Responsibilities:

- inbound AI call handling
- missed-call recovery
- urgency detection
- intent detection
- transcript generation
- recording generation
- qualification logic
- outbound follow-up actions
- SMS workflow actions

This role captures and routes communication events into the operating structure.

---

## Service Staff

`Service Staff` report to the `Service Director`.

At Stage A, service staff use manual workflow supported by the system.

Responsibilities:

- enter customer number
- receive customer context
- confirm ability to handle job
- complete service work
- report completion date
- submit comments after the job

Their reports feed operational and management logic upward.

---

## Sales Staff

`Sales Staff` report to the `Sales Director`.

At Stage A, sales staff use manual workflow supported by the system.

Responsibilities:

- enter customer number
- receive client context
- contact customer to confirm appointment timing
- conduct estimate visit or sales follow-up
- enter estimate amount
- report lead movement and outcome

Their reporting feeds revenue and management logic upward.

---

## Stage A Customer Routing Logic

Every customer enters a defined pipeline.

Stage A currently includes these major origin paths:

- `New Call`
- `Legacy Import`
- `Referral`
- `Reactivation`
- `Owner Sales Entry`
- `Owner Service Entry`

These customer origins do not sit outside the structure.

They route into the correct management path and report upward through the same hierarchy.

Examples:

- `New Call` may route toward sales or service follow-up
- `Legacy Import` routes into reactivation logic
- `Referral` routes into priority handling
- `Owner Sales Entry` routes into sales follow-up
- `Owner Service Entry` routes into service follow-up

---

## Reporting Logic to President

The President should not primarily see raw call records or staff-level noise.

The President should see:

- CEO summary
- COO operational report
- CRO revenue report
- CFO financial-truth report
- director summaries when needed
- drill-down access to supporting details

This means:

- communication activity reports upward through `Director of Communications → COO`
- revenue effect reports upward through `Sales Director → CRO`
- service completion and service execution report upward through `Service Director → COO`
- financial outcome truth reports upward through `CFO`

Then:

`COO + CRO + CFO → CEO → President`

## Stage A Active Management Blocks

For Stage A, the active command blocks should be:

### COO Block

- `COO`
- `Director of Communications`
- `Service Director`

### CRO Block

- `CRO`
- `Sales Director`

### CFO Block

- `CFO`

The CFO may later expand into a larger finance-management block, but at Stage A it can remain lean.

---

## Expansion Rule

This hierarchy must support future stage growth without breaking Stage A.

- Stage A must work independently
- Stage B must add scheduling without breaking Stage A
- Stage C must add advanced management without breaking Stage A or B

That means this hierarchy is not temporary.

It is the permanent management spine of the system.

Later stages add more capability, more directors, and more executive depth, but they do not replace the Stage A structure.

---

## Final Principle

`AI-ABC` is not a bundle of features.

It is a structured operating environment.

At Stage A, that structure already exists.

The system is already:

- assigning ownership
- routing activity
- collecting reporting
- creating executive visibility
- preparing President governance

That is why Stage A should be treated as the first live layer of a corporate operating structure, not just a call-handling release.
