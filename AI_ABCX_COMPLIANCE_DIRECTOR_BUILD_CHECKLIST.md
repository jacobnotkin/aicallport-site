## 1. Compliance Director Mission

Compliance Director is the visible rule-and-record discipline lane of AI-ABCX governance.

Its job is to:

- keep current compliance decisions visible
- attach compliance decisions to explicit records
- preserve traceability before deeper governance automation activates
- reduce informal decision-making
- keep policy drift visible
- connect visible approvals, risk notes, and policy references
- protect the President from hidden governance gaps
- keep governance discipline present even before the full CAO layer becomes deeply active

Compliance Director should not feel like a legal abstraction.

It should feel like the AI-ABCX director that says:

- what rule-sensitive decisions are currently visible
- whether those decisions are properly recorded
- where policy drift is starting
- whether traceability is strong enough for later governance depth

---

## 2. Compliance Director Launch Standard

Compliance Director is launch-ready only when it can do all of the following:

1. keep compliance-sensitive decisions visible
2. preserve explicit records and traceability
3. distinguish clean records from informal decision paths
4. show policy-drift and missing-log risk clearly
5. connect cleanly with Approval, Risk, and President visibility
6. support governance-stage discipline
7. support A / B / C behavior clearly
8. support real internal testing with visible compliance quality

If one of these is missing, the director is not complete.

## 3. Canonical Role Definition

### Public-facing name

`Compliance Director`

### Internal role meaning

The AI-ABCX director responsible for keeping compliance-relevant decisions visible, traceable, and attached to explicit records until deeper governance control becomes active.

### Executive owner

`CAO`

Compliance Director belongs to the CAO lane because it is part of the governance structure that protects decision discipline, traceability, and policy coherence.

Compliance Director should promise:

- clearer visibility into compliance-sensitive business decisions
- less hidden or informal rule handling
- stronger traceability

- better confidence that policy-sensitive actions are not becoming scattered
- more disciplined governance as the business grows

The commercial promise is not:

## 7. A / B / C Definition Draft

### Level A

Level A Compliance Director is visible compliance discipline with explicit record attachment.

Includes:

- visible compliance decisions
- tracked priority items
- owner notes
- exception visibility
- basic policy-drift visibility

Best fit:

- founder-led businesses
- early-stage AI-ABCX systems
- companies that need governance visibility without heavy automation

### Level B

Level B Compliance Director adds stronger traceability and better exception handling.

Includes everything in A, plus:

- stronger missing-log visibility
- clearer exception handling
- better cross-linkage with approvals and risk notes
- stronger traceability discipline
- more mature compliance review quality

Best fit:

- growing teams
- businesses with more people making decisions
- companies beginning to need stronger governance coordination

### Level C

Level C Compliance Director adds active governance depth and stronger audit readiness.

Includes everything in B, plus:

- stronger policy-governance enforcement
- more advanced compliance-routing logic
- deeper traceability and review discipline
- stronger support for active CAO governance
- better protection against invisible policy drift

Best fit:

- larger service businesses
- companies using broader AI-ABCX configurations
- businesses ready for stronger governance maturity

## 13. Required Handoffs

Compliance Director must hand off cleanly to:

- `Approval Director`
  - when decision routing and signoff handling are central
- `Risk Director`
  - when compliance concerns create or reveal larger governance risk
- `Executive Reporting Director`
  - when governance narrative should reflect compliance visibility
- `President`
  - when visible compliance decisions require review or confirmation

Without these handoffs, Compliance becomes an isolated record-keeping lane instead of a true governance-control director.

---

## 14. Guided Activation Requirements

The activation flow should eventually help determine:

- whether multiple people already make sensitive operational or customer-facing decisions
- whether the owner struggles to keep policy-sensitive actions visible
- whether the business already has scattered approvals or inconsistent records
- whether governance maturity matters now or mostly later
- whether the owner wants stronger traceability before deeper automation expands

This matters because Compliance Director should be recommended more strongly where business growth is starting to outrun informal memory and manual oversight.

## 15. Pricing Decisions Still Required

Pricing still needs final decisions for:

- whether Compliance Director is primarily bundled into broader governance or full-system configurations
- whether Level A is mostly visible governance discipline rather than heavy standalone software
- whether Level C should be reserved for stronger governance-ready businesses
- whether compliance should usually be sold together with Approval and Risk instead of isolated

Current practical recommendation:

- treat Compliance Director as a real launch director
- keep it available at all levels
- usually recommend it inside broader governance-ready or full-system configurations rather than as a first isolated purchase

## 16. Build Sequence

Recommended build order:

1. normalize canonical role naming
2. define compliance record structure
3. define categories and states
4. connect visible approvals and risk notes into compliance inputs
5. build President and CAO visibility
6. define traceability, exception, and drift logic
7. connect handoffs into Approval and Risk
8. define A / B / C behavior
9. test compliance visibility and record quality

This order keeps compliance grounded in real governance behavior instead of abstract policy language.
