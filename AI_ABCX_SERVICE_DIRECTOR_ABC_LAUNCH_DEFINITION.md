# AI-ABCX Service Director A/B/C Launch Definition

## 1. Core Rule

Service Director is the execution lane after work is assigned.

It is responsible for:

- active job visibility
- completion visibility
- delayed-job visibility
- service issue visibility
- field-note continuity
- backlog awareness
- follow-through visibility

It is not the same as:

- Scheduling Director, which controls appointment timing
- Dispatch Director, which controls crew movement and route coordination
- Follow-Up Director, which controls post-job unresolved action

Service Director controls the truth of what is actually happening in service execution.

---

## 2. Public Meaning Of Each Level

### Level A
Visible service execution truth.

### Level B
Connected service workflow.

### Level C
Advanced service orchestration.

---

## 3. Level A Definition

Level A Service Director means the business can clearly see:

- which service jobs are active
- which service jobs were completed
- which service jobs are delayed
- which service jobs have visible issues
- which notes were recorded from the field
- where service pressure is building

Level A does **not** require deep automation.

It is first a visibility and operational-truth layer.

At Level A:

- service work is visible
- completion truth is visible
- service problems do not disappear
- field execution can be reviewed by leadership
- the President can see operational service health

Level A is the minimum level required for real service control.

---

## 4. Level B Definition

Level B Service Director means service execution is not only visible, but more coordinated.

At Level B:

- active work is organized more clearly
- completion discipline is stronger
- issue handling is more structured
- backlog visibility is more usable
- service execution connects more clearly to adjacent lanes
- operational bottlenecks become easier to detect and manage

Level B turns service from visible work into a connected operating workflow.

This is the level where the business starts feeling that execution is being managed, not just watched.

---

## 5. Level C Definition

Level C Service Director means service becomes a high-control execution lane.

At Level C:

- service pressure is surfaced earlier
- issue handling becomes richer and more structured
- execution exceptions are handled with stronger logic
- completion risk is more visible before damage spreads
- cross-lane coordination is stronger
- the President gets deeper operational visibility into throughput and blockage

Level C is for businesses that want a more advanced service-control layer, not just service visibility.

This is the most complete version of service execution control at launch.

---

## 6. Exact Launch Difference Between A, B, And C

### A = visible service execution truth

The company can see:

- active jobs
- completed jobs
- delayed jobs
- issue-flagged jobs
- field notes
- basic operational service pressure

### B = connected service workflow

Everything in A, plus:

- stronger execution coordination
- stronger backlog discipline
- stronger issue control
- stronger completion control
- stronger service handoff continuity

### C = advanced service orchestration

Everything in B, plus:

- richer exception handling
- stronger pressure detection
- stronger cross-lane service coordination
- deeper executive visibility
- more advanced execution management logic

---

## 7. Dependency Guidance

Service Director can exist with limited independence, but it becomes stronger when paired with other directors.

### Can Service Director exist by itself?

Yes, in a limited form.

This makes sense when a company already has:

- work entering from outside systems
- manual scheduling already in place
- manual dispatch already in place
- a need for service visibility more than intake automation

### Strongest pairings

Service Director becomes much stronger with:

- Scheduling Director
- Dispatch Director
- Follow-Up Director
- Call Handling Director

### Practical rule

- Service A can stand alone more credibly
- Service B usually works best with at least one adjacent director
- Service C works best as part of a broader operating system

---

## 8. Recommendation Logic Guidance

Recommend **Service Director A** when:

- the business mainly needs visibility into active jobs
- field work is happening but not clearly visible
- jobs are getting completed without enough execution truth
- leadership needs operational clarity first

Recommend **Service Director B** when:

- service work is active enough to require stronger coordination
- backlog and issue handling are becoming harder to control
- completion discipline needs improvement
- service execution needs stronger workflow continuity

Recommend **Service Director C** when:

- the company wants a high-control service lane
- service exceptions are costly
- throughput management matters
- the business is scaling service volume
- service execution needs to coordinate tightly with the rest of the system

---

## 9. Business-Friendly Explanation For Customers

### Service Director A

You can clearly see what service work is happening, what was completed, what is delayed, and where issues are building.

### Service Director B

Your service work becomes more organized and coordinated, with stronger control over completion, backlog, and issue handling.

### Service Director C

Your service lane becomes a higher-control operating system with stronger exception handling, better pressure detection, and deeper leadership visibility.

---

## 10. What Must Be Avoided

Do not describe Service Director as:

- just another scheduling feature
- just dispatch tracking
- only a technician screen
- only completion logging

That makes it too small.

Service Director is the operating truth of execution after work is assigned.

Also avoid pretending:

- A is heavily automated
- B is the same as C
- C is only “more reports”

The difference must stay operationally meaningful.

---

## 11. Pricing Use

This definition allows pricing by control depth:

- A = visibility and service truth
- B = workflow coordination and control
- C = advanced execution orchestration

That makes pricing easier to explain because the customer is paying for a stronger service operating lane, not random feature bundles.
